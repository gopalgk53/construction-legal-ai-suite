# NTO Operations — QC Checklist

**Knowledge Material #3**
**Version:** 0.1 — Working Draft
**Purpose:** Provide a consistent checklist for reviewing a Work Order and completed notice before final processing.

---

## 1. Purpose

Quality Control (QC) verifies that the completed notice is consistent with the available customer information, approved research evidence, and established operational procedure.

QC should identify discrepancies rather than resolve uncertainty through assumptions.

---

## 2. QC Review Context

A typical Work Order reaches QC after the researcher completes preparation and submits it for review.

Typical state transition:

`Processing → Under Review`

QC may review:

- Completed Notice
- Customer-provided information
- Research attachments
- Property Card
- NOC
- Permit information
- Bond information
- Other approved supporting evidence

The completed notice and supporting evidence should be compared directly.

---

## 3. Core QC Checklist

Verify the following when applicable:

### Project Information

- [ ] Job address
- [ ] County
- [ ] City / State / ZIP
- [ ] Suite or unit information
- [ ] Job name
- [ ] Project classification/type

### Notice Information

- [ ] Appropriate notice type
- [ ] Customer information
- [ ] First day information
- [ ] Job amount
- [ ] Type of work / materials furnished
- [ ] Spelling and formatting

### Property Information

- [ ] Property owner
- [ ] Folio / parcel information
- [ ] Legal description
- [ ] Property information agrees with approved evidence

### NOC — When Applicable

- [ ] Applicable NOC attached
- [ ] NOC corresponds to the project
- [ ] Owner information reviewed
- [ ] GC information reviewed
- [ ] NOC instrument / recording information entered when required
- [ ] Relevant participants reviewed

### Bond / Public Project — When Applicable

- [ ] Applicable bond attached
- [ ] Bond corresponds to the project
- [ ] GC reviewed
- [ ] Bond company / surety reviewed
- [ ] Bond number entered when required
- [ ] Relevant participants reviewed

### Participants

- [ ] Owner
- [ ] GC / Owner-Builder when applicable
- [ ] Customer
- [ ] Customer's Customer
- [ ] Property Manager when applicable
- [ ] Tenant when applicable
- [ ] Lender when applicable
- [ ] Surety / bond-related participants when applicable
- [ ] Other required participants

### Contractual / Payment Chain

Verify that the participant hierarchy is supported by available evidence.

Do not assume a missing relationship merely to create a complete chain.

---

## 4. Evidence Checklist

Confirm that applicable research evidence has been attached to the correct area.

Examples:

### Research

- Property Card
- Maps
- Reference documents

### NOC

- Notice of Commencement
- Applicable recording / instrument information

### Bond

- Bond document
- Bond number
- Applicable recording / reference information

Evidence should correspond to the Work Order being reviewed.

---

## 5. Conflict Check

Before completing QC, check whether information conflicts across:

- Job Sheet
- Completed Notice
- Property Card
- NOC
- Permit
- Bond
- Customer / CC confirmation
- Other approved evidence

If a material conflict remains unresolved:

**Do not guess.**

Return to the documented research, confirmation, or escalation process.

---

## 6. Final QC Review

Before approving the completed notice, perform a final review of:

1. Project information
2. Notice information
3. Property information
4. Participants
5. Payment / contractual hierarchy
6. Supporting evidence
7. Spelling and formatting
8. Unresolved discrepancies

The objective is to verify that the notice is supported by the available approved information.

---

## 7. Workflow Completion

After successful QC, the completed notice may proceed according to the approved operational workflow.

Typical lifecycle:

`Under Review → Completed Notice → Mailed`

The Copilot must not claim that it approved, completed, mailed, or modified a Work Order.

---

## 8. Copilot Behavior

When asked:

> "What should I check before submitting this WO to QC?"

NTO Operations Copilot should provide the relevant checklist in a clear sequence.

When the user describes a specific issue, the Copilot should emphasize the applicable QC checks rather than unnecessarily returning the entire checklist.

If information conflicts or cannot be established from approved knowledge, the Copilot should identify the uncertainty and direct the employee to the documented research or escalation procedure.

The Copilot must never fabricate missing evidence or claim that QC has been completed.
