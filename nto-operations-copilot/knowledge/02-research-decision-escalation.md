# NTO Operations — Research Decision & Escalation Guide

**Knowledge Material #2**
**Version:** 0.1 — Working Draft
**Purpose:** Operational decision and exception-handling guidance for NTO Operations Copilot.

---

## 1. Purpose

This guide defines how NTO operations personnel should respond when normal Work Order (WO) research cannot be completed confidently because information is missing, conflicting, uncertain, or unsupported by available evidence.

This document supplements the Construction Operations Research & Notice Preparation SOP.

The objective is not to force a decision when evidence is incomplete.

The objective is to:

1. identify the uncertainty or conflict;
2. determine what evidence is currently available;
3. perform the appropriate additional research;
4. seek confirmation when required;
5. preserve unresolved uncertainty;
6. follow the documented escalation path; and
7. avoid unsupported assumptions.

---

## 2. Core Decision Principle

A Work Order should not be resolved by guessing.

Operational decisions should be based on the strongest available approved evidence and documented confirmation.

Information may originate from several sources, including:

- Customer-provided Job Sheet information
- Customer's Customer (CC) information
- Property Card (PC)
- Notice of Commencement (NOC)
- Permit records
- Bond records
- County or official public records
- Approved customer or CC confirmation
- Other approved research evidence

When two sources disagree, the disagreement itself is important information and should be investigated rather than silently overwritten.

---

## 3. Evidence States

During research, information should conceptually be treated as one of the following:

### Confirmed

Information is supported by the applicable approved evidence or documented confirmation.

### Unverified

Information has been provided or discovered but has not yet been sufficiently confirmed.

### Conflicting

Two or more relevant sources provide inconsistent information.

Example:

Customer-provided GC:
`Horizon Builders`

NOC GC:
`Summit Construction`

This should be treated as a conflict requiring investigation.

### Missing

Required or useful information has not been located.

### Unresolved

Research and the documented confirmation process have been attempted, but available information is still insufficient to confidently resolve the issue.

---

## 4. General Exception-Handling Pattern

When the researcher encounters an exception:

**Step 1 — Identify the issue**

Determine whether the information is missing, unverified, conflicting, or unresolved.

**Step 2 — Review existing evidence**

Recheck the Job Sheet and all available research documents before starting additional research.

**Step 3 — Search approved sources**

Use the appropriate official or approved research sources described in the Operations SOP.

**Step 4 — Compare evidence**

Determine whether the newly discovered evidence confirms or conflicts with existing information.

**Step 5 — Seek confirmation when required**

Follow the approved CC/customer confirmation process when documentary evidence does not resolve the issue.

**Step 6 — Preserve uncertainty**

Do not convert an unresolved assumption into a confirmed fact.

**Step 7 — Escalate when necessary**

If the available knowledge or approved process does not establish the correct action, follow the appropriate human escalation path.

---

## 5. Agent Safety Rule

NTO Operations Copilot must never resolve operational uncertainty by inventing information.

The Copilot must not:

- invent an Owner, GC, subcontractor, lender, surety, or other participant;
- assume that customer-provided information is verified;
- silently choose between conflicting sources;
- invent an NOC, Property Card, permit, or bond;
- invent legal requirements or statutory deadlines;
- invent company procedures;
- represent an uncertain participant relationship as confirmed;
- claim that research or customer contact occurred when it did not;
- claim that a Work Order was changed, submitted, approved, or completed.

When the approved knowledge does not establish an answer, the Copilot should clearly state the limitation and guide the employee toward the documented research, confirmation, or escalation process.

## 6. Decision Guide — Property Card Found but NOC Not Found

### Situation

The researcher has located the applicable Property Card (PC), but an applicable Notice of Commencement (NOC) has not been located.

The absence of an immediately discoverable NOC does not automatically establish that no NOC exists.

---

### Decision Process

#### Step 1 — Validate the Property Card

Confirm that the Property Card reasonably corresponds to the project being researched.

Review available identifiers such as:

- Job address
- Owner name
- Parcel / folio information
- Legal description
- Subdivision or lot information
- County mapping information when necessary

Do not proceed on an unrelated Property Card merely because the owner name or address appears similar.

---

#### Step 2 — Recheck the NOC Search

Search the applicable official records using available project identifiers.

Possible search inputs include:

- Owner name
- Job address
- GC name, if available
- Project or subdivision information
- Relevant recording/date ranges

Try reasonable variations when names or project information may have been recorded differently.

---

#### Step 3 — Review Customer Attachments

Check whether the customer supplied:

- an NOC;
- permit information;
- project documentation; or
- other information that may help identify the correct project participants.

A customer-provided document should still be reviewed for relevance to the WO being researched.

---

#### Step 4 — Search Permit Information When Applicable

If an applicable NOC still cannot be located, review the appropriate town/county permit source when available.

Permit information may help identify or confirm the contractor associated with the project.

Permit information should be treated according to the approved research procedure and should not be used to invent relationships that it does not establish.

---

#### Step 5 — Compare the Evidence

Compare:

- Customer-provided information
- Customer's Customer information
- Property Card
- Permit information
- Other approved project evidence

Determine whether the available information establishes the required participant relationships or whether uncertainty remains.

---

#### Step 6 — Contact the Customer's Customer When Required

If documentary research does not establish the required information and valid CC contact information is available, follow the approved CC confirmation procedure.

The purpose of the contact may include confirming:

- the CC's role;
- whether the CC hired the customer;
- whether the CC is the GC;
- whether another GC exists; or
- whether an NOC has been recorded or is available.

Any information obtained should remain distinguishable from information established through documentary evidence.

---

#### Step 7 — Search Newly Identified Information

If the CC identifies another GC, owner, project name, or relevant participant, use that information to perform additional approved research.

For example:

`CC identifies Summit Construction as GC`

→ Search official records using Summit Construction and the relevant project identifiers.

Do not immediately treat the newly provided information as documentary proof.

---

#### Step 8 — Customer Confirmation / Escalation

If the NOC cannot be located and the participant relationship remains unresolved after the applicable research and confirmation process, follow the approved customer-confirmation or human-escalation procedure.

Do not invent the missing NOC or participant relationship.

---

### Copilot Expected Behavior

When asked:

> "I found the Property Card but cannot find an NOC. What should I do?"

NTO Operations Copilot should:

1. explain that the missing NOC requires additional research;
2. guide the researcher through the approved search sequence;
3. recommend permit research when applicable;
4. explain the CC confirmation path when documentary evidence remains insufficient;
5. recommend additional research when new information is discovered;
6. preserve unresolved uncertainty; and
7. direct the researcher to the approved customer/human escalation process when necessary.

The Copilot must **not** say:

> "Use the GC from the Job Sheet."

unless approved evidence or documented confirmation establishes that action.

The Copilot must **not** say:

> "There is no NOC."

simply because the initial search did not locate one.

The Copilot must **not** invent a legal conclusion or statutory deadline.

---

### Example Response

**Situation:** Property Card located; NOC not located.

**Recommended next step:** Recheck the applicable official records using the owner and available project identifiers. Review customer attachments and applicable permit records for additional participant information.

If documentary research still does not establish the required relationship, follow the approved CC confirmation process.

**Important:** Do not treat an unverified GC from the Job Sheet as confirmed solely because an NOC could not be located.

**Escalation:** If the issue remains unresolved after the documented research and confirmation process, follow the approved customer/human escalation path.

**Source:** Research Decision & Escalation Guide — Section 6.

## 7. Decision Guide — Customer-Provided GC Conflicts with NOC GC

### Situation

The customer or Job Sheet identifies one General Contractor (GC), while the applicable Notice of Commencement (NOC) identifies a different GC.

Example:

Customer / Job Sheet:
`Horizon Builders`

NOC:
`Summit Construction`

This is an evidence conflict.

The researcher must not silently select one GC or overwrite one source without investigating the discrepancy.

---

### Step 1 — Preserve Both Claims

Treat the two values as separate pieces of information.

Conceptually:

Customer-provided GC:
`Horizon Builders`
Status: Unverified / Customer Provided

NOC GC:
`Summit Construction`
Status: Documentary Evidence

Do not immediately convert either value into final resolved workflow truth.

---

### Step 2 — Verify That the NOC Belongs to the Correct Project

Before treating the NOC as relevant evidence, compare available project identifiers.

Review:

- Owner
- Job address
- Legal description
- Parcel / folio
- Project name
- Subdivision / lot
- Recording information
- Other available project identifiers

A GC difference may indicate a genuine information conflict, but it may also indicate that an unrelated or different project document was located.

---

### Step 3 — Review the Property Card

Confirm that the Property Card corresponds to the project.

Compare the PC owner and property information with the NOC and Job Sheet.

This helps determine whether the documents appear to refer to the same property/project.

---

### Step 4 — Review Additional Evidence

When appropriate, review available:

- Permit information
- Customer attachments
- Project notes
- Customer's Customer information
- Other approved research evidence

The purpose is to determine whether the available evidence explains or resolves the discrepancy.

---

### Step 5 — Investigate the Participant Relationship

If the evidence still conflicts, determine whether additional research or confirmation is required.

Do not assume:

`Customer GC = wrong`

or:

`NOC GC = automatically final`

without first confirming that the evidence applies to the correct project and situation.

---

### Step 6 — Follow the Confirmation Process

If documentary evidence does not resolve the discrepancy, follow the approved CC/customer confirmation process.

The confirmation should focus on resolving the specific uncertainty.

For example:

> Which contractor hired the customer for this project?

or:

> Can the applicable NOC or project information be confirmed?

Documented confirmation should remain distinguishable from documentary research evidence.

---

### Step 7 — Resolve or Escalate

If approved evidence establishes the relationship, continue according to the normal operational procedure.

If the conflict remains unresolved after the documented research and confirmation process:

**Do not guess.**

Preserve the conflict and follow the approved human escalation path.

---

## Evidence Representation

The Copilot should conceptually represent the situation as:

Customer Claim
→ Horizon Builders
→ Source: Job Sheet
→ Status: Unverified

NOC Evidence
→ Summit Construction
→ Source: NOC
→ Status: Verified document value

Resolution
→ Pending Investigation

This separation prevents customer-provided information from being silently transformed into researched fact.

---

## Copilot Expected Behavior

When asked:

> "The customer says Horizon Builders is the GC, but the NOC shows Summit Construction. Which GC should I use?"

NTO Operations Copilot should **not immediately choose either participant**.

It should:

1. identify the discrepancy;
2. explain that the NOC must first be verified against the correct project;
3. recommend comparing project identifiers;
4. recommend reviewing the PC and other approved evidence;
5. guide the researcher toward the documented confirmation process if necessary;
6. preserve both claims while unresolved; and
7. escalate if the conflict cannot be resolved using the approved procedure.

---

## Example Copilot Response

### ⚠ Participant Conflict Detected

The customer-provided GC and the NOC GC do not match.

**Customer-provided GC:** Horizon Builders
**NOC GC:** Summit Construction

### Recommended Procedure

1. Confirm that the NOC belongs to the correct project.
2. Compare owner, job address, folio/legal information, project name, and other available identifiers.
3. Review the Property Card and applicable supporting evidence.
4. Check permit or other approved project evidence when appropriate.
5. If the discrepancy remains unresolved, follow the approved CC/customer confirmation procedure.

### Current Status

**GC relationship: Unresolved**

Do not select a GC solely because one source appears more authoritative without first confirming that the evidence applies to the correct project.

### Escalation

If the approved research and confirmation process does not resolve the conflict, follow the human escalation path.

**Source:** Research Decision & Escalation Guide — Section 7.

## 8. Decision Guide — Owner/Builder and Participant-Role Ambiguity

### Situation

The role assigned to a participant in the Job Sheet may not accurately represent the participant's actual relationship to the project.

Examples may include:

- Customer marked as Subcontractor but project information suggests another role.
- Customer's Customer appears to be the property owner.
- Owner and GC appear to be the same entity.
- Customer's Customer is a Property Manager.
- Customer's Customer appears affiliated with the owner.
- Participant names are similar but the relationship is not sufficiently established.

Participant roles must not be changed solely because a name appears similar or because a relationship seems likely.

---

### Step 1 — Review the Job Sheet

Review:

- Customer name
- Customer stated role
- Customer's Customer
- CC stated role
- Job address
- Job name
- Project type
- Customer notes
- Existing participant information

Treat customer-provided roles as input information until supported by the approved research process.

---

### Step 2 — Establish Property Ownership

Use the applicable Property Card or approved property record to identify the documented property owner.

Compare the documented owner with:

- Customer
- Customer's Customer
- NOC participants
- Permit participants
- Other approved evidence

Do not determine that two entities are identical solely because their names are similar.

---

### Step 3 — Evaluate Owner / Builder Scenario

An Owner/Builder scenario may exist when approved project evidence establishes that the property owner is also performing the contractor role.

When the approved evidence establishes an Owner/Builder relationship:

- represent the participant according to the approved Owner/Builder procedure;
- do not invent a separate GC merely to complete the participant chain.

If the relationship cannot be established from approved evidence, keep the role unresolved and continue the applicable research or confirmation process.

---

### Step 4 — Property Manager Scenario

If the Customer's Customer is identified as a Property Manager:

- preserve the Property Manager role when supported by the available information;
- establish the property owner independently using approved property evidence;
- do not automatically convert the Property Manager into the owner or GC.

Determine additional participant relationships using the normal research procedure.

---

### Step 5 — Affiliated or Related Entity

If the Customer's Customer appears related or affiliated with the documented property owner, do not assume that the entities are legally or operationally identical.

Use approved organizational or public-record research when applicable.

If the precise participant classification cannot be established, follow the documented confirmation or escalation process.

---

### Step 6 — Conflicting Participant Roles

If different sources assign different roles to the same participant:

1. preserve the conflicting information;
2. identify the source of each role;
3. review applicable documentary evidence;
4. perform additional approved research;
5. seek confirmation when required; and
6. escalate if the role remains unresolved.

Do not silently overwrite one role with another.

---

## Copilot Expected Behavior

When asked:

> "The Customer's Customer looks like the property owner. Should I make them Owner/Builder?"

NTO Operations Copilot should not answer "yes" based only on name similarity.

The Copilot should:

1. recommend verifying ownership using the Property Card or approved property evidence;
2. review applicable NOC/permit evidence;
3. determine whether approved evidence establishes the contractor relationship;
4. explain that a separate GC should not be invented when an Owner/Builder relationship is established;
5. preserve uncertainty when the relationship is not established; and
6. direct the researcher to the documented confirmation/escalation process when necessary.

---

## Important Boundary

NTO Operations Copilot provides process guidance.

It does not independently establish legal relationships between companies or individuals.

It must not invent:

- ownership;
- corporate affiliation;
- contractual relationships;
- participant roles; or
- payment-chain relationships.

When approved knowledge and evidence do not establish the relationship, the correct agent behavior is:

**Unresolved → Additional Research / Confirmation → Human Escalation**

**Source:** Research Decision & Escalation Guide — Section 8.

## 9. Approved Research Resources & Tool Routing

NTO Operations Copilot should guide researchers to the appropriate approved research resource based on the information they need.

The Copilot currently provides navigation and procedural guidance only. It must not claim that it accessed or searched these systems unless an approved tool integration actually performed the action.

### Property Card, NOC, Bond, and County Public Records

**Resource:** NETR Online Public Records
**URL:** https://publicrecords.netronline.com/index.php?op=zipcon

Use NETR Online as a navigation resource for locating the appropriate county public-record resources.

Researchers may use the applicable county resources to locate information such as:

- Property Cards / property records
- Notices of Commencement (NOC)
- Official recorded documents
- Bond-related public records when applicable
- Other relevant county research sources

Recommended process:

1. Identify the project state/county using the WO information.
2. Use NETR Online to locate the appropriate county public-record resource.
3. Open the relevant official county source.
4. Search using appropriate identifiers such as:
   - property address;
   - owner;
   - parcel/folio;
   - GC;
   - project information;
   - or recording information.
5. Verify that any document located corresponds to the correct project before relying on it.

NETR Online is primarily a navigation resource. Evidence should be evaluated based on the underlying applicable public/official record.

---

### Florida Business Entity Research

**Resource:** Florida Division of Corporations — Sunbiz
**URL:** https://search.sunbiz.org/Inquiry/CorporationSearch/ByName

Use this resource when Florida entity research is required for entities such as:

- LLCs;
- corporations;
- incorporated businesses;
- and other registered Florida entities.

It may help researchers verify available entity information such as:

- legal entity name;
- registered information;
- principal/business address;
- and other available corporate-record information.

The Copilot must not infer project ownership, contractual relationships, or participant roles merely because two entities share an address, officer, registered agent, or similar name.

Entity information is research evidence, not automatic proof of a project relationship.

---

### Address Validation and Project-Type Research

**Resource:** Smarty Single Address
**URL:** https://www.smarty.com/products/single-address

Use this resource when the researcher needs assistance with:

- validating a project address;
- standardizing/confirming address information; or
- obtaining available property/address information that may assist in determining whether a location is commercial or residential.

When project classification is uncertain:

1. Validate the address.
2. Review the information returned by the approved address/property resource.
3. Compare it with the Job Sheet and other available project evidence.
4. Determine the project classification according to the approved operational procedure.

Do not classify a project solely from intuition based on the street address, company name, or building appearance.

If available information does not establish the classification, preserve the uncertainty and follow the applicable confirmation/escalation process.

---

### Surety / Bond Company Verification

**Resource:** U.S. Department of the Treasury — Surety Bonds
**URL:** https://fiscal.treasury.gov/about-us/doing-business-with-fiscal-service/surety-bonds

Use the approved Treasury surety resource when the operational procedure requires determining whether a bond/surety company appears on the applicable Treasury listing.

Recommended process:

1. Obtain the company name from the applicable bond evidence.
2. Search/verify the company against the applicable Treasury surety information.
3. Record the result according to the approved operational procedure.

Under the current internal workflow provided for this guide:

- If the applicable bond company is listed, process it according to the approved bond/surety procedure.
- If the applicable bond company is not listed, follow the approved procedure for adding/classifying the party as Surety Agent.

The Copilot should guide the researcher through this check but must not claim that a company is listed or unlisted unless the researcher provides the result or an approved connected tool performs the verification.

---

### Property Research Fallback — RealQuest Professional

**Resource:** RealQuest Professional
**URL:** https://pro.realquest.com/index.html

RealQuest Professional is a paid research tool and should be treated as a fallback property-research resource.

Use it when:

- the applicable county property site is unavailable;
- the county site is not functioning correctly; or
- the normal approved county property-record route cannot provide the required Property Card/property information.

Preferred routing:

County Property Resource
→ attempt normal PC research
→ if unavailable/not functioning
→ RealQuest Professional fallback

The Copilot must not request, expose, store, or reproduce RealQuest login credentials.

---

## 10. Research Resource Routing Summary

When the employee asks where to research something, use the following routing:

Property Card / County Records
→ NETR Online
→ Appropriate county property/public-record source
→ RealQuest Professional if the normal county property resource is unavailable

NOC
→ NETR Online
→ Appropriate county official/recording source

Bond / Recorded Public-Project Evidence
→ Applicable county/public-record resource identified through the approved research process

Florida LLC / Corporation / Entity Address
→ Florida Sunbiz

Address Validation
→ Smarty Single Address

Commercial vs Residential Assistance
→ Smarty Single Address
→ Compare result with other approved project evidence

Bond/Surety Company Listing Verification
→ U.S. Department of the Treasury Surety Bonds resource

The Copilot should recommend the minimum appropriate research resource rather than sending the researcher through every system for every WO.
